# AI-Assisted Concept Learning Workflow

**Goal:** Build deep intuition first, then develop problem-solving ability.

Use this workflow for **every new concept** (Probability, Statistics, Linear Algebra, Deep Learning, etc.).

---

## Step 1 — Learn the Concept

Read the textbook or watch a lecture.

Ask:

- What problem is this concept trying to solve?
    
- Why does it exist?
    
- What does each symbol mean?
    
- What is the intuition?
    

**Output:** A one-sentence explanation in your own words.

---

## Step 2 — Build Intuition

Ask AI:

> Explain this concept intuitively.

Request:

- Everyday analogy
    
- ASCII diagram
    
- Real-world example
    
- Tiny example (dice, cards, coins, students)
    
- Common misconceptions
    

Do **not** memorize the formula yet.

**Goal:** Build a mental picture.

---

## Step 3 — Translation Practice (Most Important)

Translate between all three representations.

### English → Symbols

Example:

> Probability that a student passed given the student is female.

↓

`P(Pass | Female)`

---

### Symbols → English

Example:

`P(A ∩ B)`

↓

Probability that **both A and B happen.**

---

### Symbols → Picture

Example:

`P(A | B)`

↓

Draw the sample space.

Shrink the universe to **B**.

Measure how much of **B** belongs to **A**.

**Goal:** Learn to think in multiple representations.

---

## Step 4 — Recognition Practice

Ask AI for 20 quick recognition questions.

Examples:

- Is this Union or Intersection?
    
- Is this Conditional Probability?
    
- Is this Complement?
    
- Are these events independent?
    
- Do these events overlap?
    
- Is one event a subset of another?
    

Do **not** calculate anything.

**Goal:** Instantly recognize patterns.

---

## Step 5 — Visual Practice

Ask AI to generate visual reasoning exercises.

Examples:

- Draw A ∩ B.
    
- Draw A ∪ B.
    
- Draw Aᶜ.
    
- Draw disjoint events.
    
- Draw a partition.
    
- Draw P(A|B).
    

**Goal:** Think in pictures instead of formulas.

---

## Step 6 — Tiny Numerical Exercises

Only now begin calculations.

Use very small examples.

Examples:

- One die
    
- Two coins
    
- Six cards
    
- Ten students
    

Avoid large datasets initially.

**Goal:** Connect intuition with mathematics.

---

## Step 7 — Teach It Back

Close the book.

Explain the concept aloud or in writing.

Do not use formulas unless necessary.

If you cannot explain it simply, revisit the intuition.

**Goal:** Verify genuine understanding.

---

## Step 8 — Connect the Concept

Ask:

- Where is this used?
    
- Which future topics depend on it?
    
- How does it appear in Machine Learning?
    
- How does it appear in Deep Learning?
    
- How does it appear in LLMs?
    

**Goal:** Build a connected knowledge graph instead of isolated facts.

---

# The Six Questions for Every New Concept

Whenever you learn something new, answer these questions:

1. What question does this concept answer?
    
2. What does every symbol represent?
    
3. Can I draw it?
    
4. Can I explain it in plain English?
    
5. Can I solve one small example?
    
6. Where will I use this later?
    

If you cannot answer one of these, your understanding is incomplete.

---

# The Learning Loop

```text
Learn

Build Intuition

↓

Translate

↓

Recognize

↓

Visualize

↓

Solve

↓

Teach

↓

Connect

↓

Repeat
```

---

# Core Principle

**Don't memorize formulas.**

Instead:

- Understand the question.
    
- Understand the symbols.
    
- Build a mental picture.
    
- Practice translating between English, symbols, and diagrams.
    
- Solve small problems.
    
- Explain the idea in your own words.
    

Once the meaning is clear, the formulas become much easier to remember and use.

I think this workflow aligns very well with how you naturally learn. Rather than trying to change your learning style, it gives it structure while ensuring you don't skip the practice that turns understanding into long-term skill.
