# Statistics: Two Ways of Thinking

Statistics has **two jobs**.

```
Statistics
│
├── Descriptive Statistics
└── Inferential Statistics
```

They answer two different questions.

---

# Descriptive Statistics — "Describe What You Can See"

Think like a **photographer** 📷.

You have the complete dataset in front of you.

Your job is **not** to guess or predict.

Your job is simply to **describe the data**.

> **"Describe this crowd."**

### Purpose

Understand and summarize the data you already have.

### Questions it answers

- Where is the center?
    
- Who is the middle person?
    
- What is most common?
    
- How spread out is the crowd?
    
- What shape does the crowd make?
    

### Tools

- Mean → **Balance Point**
    
- Median → **Middle Person**
    
- Mode → **Most Common**
    
- Variance → **Messiness**
    
- Standard Deviation → **Typical Distance from Center**
    
- Distribution → **Shape of the Crowd**
    

These together give a complete picture of the dataset.

---

# Inferential Statistics — "Learn About What You Cannot See"

Think like a **detective** 🕵️.

You cannot observe the whole population.

You only have a **sample**.

Your job is to use that evidence to learn about the larger population.

> **"Can this small group tell me about the much bigger group?"**

### Purpose

Draw conclusions about a population using a sample.

```
Reality (Too Big)
        │
        ▼
Take a Sample
        │
        ▼
Collect Evidence
        │
        ▼
Analyze
        │
        ▼
Draw Conclusions
```

### Questions it answers

- What is the likely average of the population?
    
- Will this medicine probably work?
    
- Is this coin fair?
    
- Is Model A better than Model B?
    
- Will this prediction likely hold for new data?
    

### Main Tasks

```
Inferential Statistics
│
├── Estimate unknown values
├── Compare groups
├── Test hypotheses
├── Measure confidence
└── Make predictions
```

Inference is **not random guessing**.

It is **evidence-based reasoning under uncertainty**.

Because we only observe a sample, the language is always:

- probably
    
- likely
    
- estimate
    
- confidence
    
- evidence

"I use limited evidence to understand a bigger reality."

---

# One-Line Summary

|Descriptive Statistics|Inferential Statistics|
|---|---|
|Describe the data you have.|Learn about the data you don't have.|
|Works on observed data.|Works from a sample to a population.|
|Summarizes.|Estimates, compares, tests, predicts.|
|No guessing.|Evidence-based conclusions with measured uncertainty.|

---

# AI Connection

Before training an AI model:

→ **Descriptive Statistics**

- Understand the dataset.
    
- Find its center, spread, and shape.
    

After training:

→ **Inferential Statistics**

- Will the model work on unseen data?
    
- Is the improvement real or due to chance?
    
- How confident are we in the results?
