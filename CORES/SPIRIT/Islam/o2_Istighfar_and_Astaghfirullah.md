# Repentance (Istighfar): The Journey from the Illusion of Self to the Reality of Allah

Most people think Istighfar means saying:

> _Astaghfirullah_ — "I seek forgiveness from Allah."

They imagine it as a spiritual broom used to clean sins after they occur.

The Qur'an teaches more.

The Prophets taught more.

The great Sufi masters discovered even more.

For them, Istighfar was not merely a response to wrongdoing. It was a way of seeing reality itself.

It was the secret by which the servant awakens from forgetfulness and returns to the awareness of Allah.

## The Forgotten Secret of Sin

The deepest problem of human beings is not sin.

The deepest problem is forgetfulness.

The Qur'an repeatedly describes humanity as forgetful. We become absorbed in ourselves, our plans, our fears, our desires, and our achievements. Gradually, we begin to live as though we possess our own power.

The ego whispers:

"I achieved this."

"I understand."

"I control my future."

"I am independent."

This is the root veil.

The outward sins are merely symptoms.

Just as smoke indicates a hidden fire, sins indicate a deeper forgetfulness of Allah.

Thus Istighfar is not merely asking forgiveness for actions.

It is returning from forgetfulness to remembrance.

The sinner thinks he is repenting from a deed.

The knower realizes he is repenting from heedlessness.

## The Meaning of "Covering"

The Arabic root _ghafara_ means "to cover."

Most people hear this and imagine Allah covering their sins from punishment.

The Sufis saw another dimension.

Sin exposes a person's poverty, weakness, and fragmentation.

The ego wants to appear complete.

Istighfar is the moment a servant admits:

"I am not complete."

"I am not self-sufficient."

"I need Allah."

The astonishing paradox is that Divine covering descends precisely when self-sufficiency is abandoned.

The wound becomes the doorway.

The crack becomes the place where mercy enters.

The servant becomes whole not by proving strength but by admitting weakness.

This is why the Qur'an repeatedly praises humility and brokenness before Allah.

A heart that knows its need is already closer to Allah than a heart impressed by itself.

## The First Station: Seeing the Sin

The beginner repents from actions.

He repents from anger.

He repents from lying.

He repents from envy.

He repents from neglecting prayer.

This stage is necessary.

Yet the Sufis say this is only the surface.

The outward act is rarely the true disease.

It is the visible branch of an invisible root.

Behind anger may be pride.

Behind envy may be insecurity.

Behind greed may be fear.

Behind showing off may be hunger for approval.

Thus sincere Istighfar gradually moves from observing actions to observing the heart.

## The Second Station: Seeing the Ego

According to many Sufi teachers, including Abu Hamid al-Ghazali, true spiritual work begins when a person stops merely observing behavior and starts observing the one producing the behavior.

The servant begins to notice patterns.

He notices that praise affects him.

Criticism disturbs him.

Success inflates him.

Failure crushes him.

He begins to see the machinery of the ego.

This is why advanced seekers often appear more aware of their faults than ordinary people.

Not because they have become worse.

Because they have become more awake.

A dusty room appears clean in darkness.

Only sunlight reveals the dust.

Likewise, Divine light reveals hidden flaws.

The discovery of faults is not regression.

It is illumination.

## The Third Station: Repenting from the Self

Here the teachings of the great Sufis become subtle.

Many masters such as Junayd al-Baghdadi taught that every spiritual state can become an idol.

A person may become proud of knowledge.

Then proud of worship.

Then proud of humility.

Then proud of repentance.

The ego survives by changing masks.

When one mask is removed, another appears.

A person says:

"I am becoming spiritual."

"I am progressing."

"I am sincere."

The Sufis warn that the "I" hidden inside these statements may be more dangerous than obvious sins.

At this stage, Istighfar becomes repentance from self-admiration.

The servant begins to ask forgiveness not only for bad deeds but also for secretly attributing goodness to himself.

## The Fourth Station: Witnessing Divine Mercy

A profound Qur'anic mystery appears in the verse:

> "Then He turned toward them so that they might repent."

The order is astonishing.

Allah's turning comes before their turning.

According to many spiritual commentators, repentance itself is evidence that Allah has already shown mercy.

The desire to return is already a gift.

The tears are a gift.

The regret is a gift.

The longing is a gift.

The opening of the heart is a gift.

What appears to be your movement toward Allah is, in reality, Allah drawing you toward Himself.

This realization transforms repentance from fear into love.

The servant no longer sees Allah as merely a Judge waiting to punish.

He sees Allah as the One constantly inviting him home.

## The Mystery of Poverty (Faqr)

Among the deepest teachings of Sufism is _faqr_—spiritual poverty.

This does not mean material poverty.

It means realizing that every quality we possess is borrowed.

Our intelligence is borrowed.

Our health is borrowed.

Our strength is borrowed.

Our life is borrowed.

Even our ability to remember Allah is borrowed.

The ego claims ownership.

The heart of the knower witnesses dependence.

Istighfar gradually strips away false ownership.

It teaches:

"You own nothing absolutely."

"You control less than you imagine."

"You are sustained at every moment."

This realization is not depressing.

It is liberating.

The burden of pretending to be God disappears.

## The Highest Istighfar

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ sought forgiveness constantly despite being protected from major sin.

Why?

The Sufis offered a beautiful answer.

Ordinary people seek forgiveness for sins.

The righteous seek forgiveness for heedlessness.

The saints seek forgiveness for witnessing themselves.

The prophets seek forgiveness because no finite creature can fully honor the Infinite Reality of Allah.

Every moment reveals a greater perfection of Allah.

Compared to that perfection, even one's highest worship feels incomplete.

Not worthless.

But incomplete.

Thus Istighfar continues forever.

Not because Allah is distant.

But because His perfection has no limit.

## The Secret Hidden in Astaghfirullah

When the tongue says:

> Astaghfirullah

The beginner means:

"Forgive my sins."

The seeker means:

"Purify my heart."

The lover means:

"Remove every veil between me and You."

The knower means:

"Save me from seeing myself apart from Your sustaining mercy."

At its deepest level, Istighfar is not simply asking Allah to erase a mistake.

It is asking Allah to remove every illusion that prevents us from seeing reality as it truly is.

And reality is this:

Allah alone is Self-Sufficient.

Allah alone is Permanent.

Allah alone is Absolute.

Everything else exists through Him, depends upon Him, and returns to Him.

Thus the journey of Istighfar is ultimately the journey from the illusion of independence to the truth of servanthood; from forgetfulness to remembrance; from ego to humility; from self-reliance to trust; and from the prison of the self to the infinite mercy of Allah.

The saints did not make Istighfar the heartbeat of spiritual life because they sinned more than others.

They made it the heartbeat of spiritual life because they saw reality more clearly than others.

The clearer they saw Allah, the smaller they saw themselves.

And the smaller they saw themselves, the greater became their love, gratitude, awe, and repentance.

A note of balance: much of the essay above reflects mainstream Qur'anic teachings interpreted through classical Sufi spirituality. However, some later Sufi expressions such as "illusion of self" should not be understood as "you do not exist." Classical Sunni scholars, including Abu Hamid al-Ghazali, Ibn al-Qayyim, and Junayd al-Baghdadi, maintained that creation is real but utterly dependent upon Allah. The deepest realization is not that "I do not exist," but rather:

> "I exist only because Allah continuously sustains my existence."

That distinction keeps the mystical insights firmly rooted in the Qur'an and orthodox Islamic theology.
